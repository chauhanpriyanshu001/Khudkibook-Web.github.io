Design Visualization Syllabus — Unit-wise Content

The official GTU curriculum for Design Visualization (subject code 4365901) as prescribed for TD · Semester 6 divides the course into 5 units, covering 24 listed topics. The unit-wise breakdown below is read from the GTU syllabus document for this subject code, so you can check what the course actually covers before deciding which papers to attempt.

Download the official syllabus PDF (4365901)

Unit 1 An overview of textiles and textile design from fibre to product purchase

  • 1.1. The global textile and design in textile industries.
  • 1.2. Textile materials, processes and products.
  • 1.3. Designers found in the textiles and clothing industries.

Unit 2 Principle and elements of textile design

  • 1.1. History of design visualization practices in textile.
  • 1.2. Balance, Movement, Layout, Grids, Repetition, contrast and unity in designs.
  • 1.3. Different types of line, shape, and form in textile design.
  • 1.4. Color, color mixing, color style color psychology, hue value, Chroma, textures and weave. Design Visualization 4365901 Topics and Sub-topics
  • 1.5. History, story, architecture both natural and manmade objects.

Unit 3 Textile materials and methods.

  • 3.1. Uses of different fibre for yarn manufacturing.
  • 3.2. Combination weave, colour, yarn selection fabric specifications and fabric finishing.
  • 3.3. Yarn, Knit structure and sewing for garment.
  • 3.4. Different yarn, colour and colour combination ,Needle and motifs for embroidery
  • 3.5. Motifs, color theme, different techniques for printing and painting textiles.
  • 3.6. Different non-woven textiles, Laces and Technical textile design methods.

Unit 4 Textile designer and the textile design functions

  • 4.1. The purpose of the textile designer.
  • 4.2. Stylist, colorist and repeat artist.
  • 4.3. In-house and freelance designs.
  • 4.3. Seasonal ranges for textile manufacturing.
  • 4.5. From sketchbook to fabric sample.
  • 4.6. Design adaption and modification.

Unit 5 Professional practices and commercial aspects of design

  • 5.2. Goslett’s model for design administration.
  • 5.3. Do’s and Don’ts for meeting.
  • 5.4. Different types of fees.
  • 5.5. Function of professional bodies
