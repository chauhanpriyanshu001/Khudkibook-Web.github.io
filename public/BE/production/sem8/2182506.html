Managing Projects Syllabus — Unit-wise Content

The official GTU curriculum for Managing Projects (subject code 2182506) as prescribed for Production · Semester 8 divides the course into 7 units, covering 40 listed topics. The unit-wise breakdown below is read from the GTU syllabus document for this subject code, so you can check what the course actually covers before deciding which papers to attempt.

Download the official syllabus PDF (2182506)

Unit 1 The world of Project Management

6 lecture hours

  • Definition of project
  • Project Management Vs
  • General Management
  • Three goals of project
  • The life cycle of projects
  • Selecting projects to meet organizational goals
  • Confronting Uncertainty
  • Project portfolio process
  • An approach to Project Formulation

Unit 2 Organizing the project

6 lecture hours

  • The PM’s Roles
  • The PM’s responsibility to the project
  • Selection of a Project Manager
  • Project Management as a profession
  • Fitting projects into the parent organization
  • The project team

Unit 3 Planning the project

8 lecture hours

  • The contents of a project plan
  • The planning process-overview
  • The planning process- Nuts and Bolts
  • The work breakdown structure and other aids
  • Multidisciplinary Teams-Balancing Pleasure and Pain

Unit 4 Budgeting the Project

6 lecture hours

  • Methods of budgeting
  • Cost estimating
  • Improving Cost Estimates
  • Budget Uncertainty and risk management

Unit 5 Scheduling the Project

8 lecture hours

  • PERT and CPM Networks
  • Project uncertainty and risk management
  • The Gantt chart
  • Extensions to PERT/CPM

Unit 6 Allocating the Resources to the Project

6 lecture hours

  • Expediting a Project
  • Resource Loading
  • Resource Leveling
  • Allocating Scarce resources to projects
  • Allocating several resources to the several projects
  • Goldratt’s critical chain

Unit 7 Monitoring and Controlling the Project

6 lecture hours

  • The plan-monitor-control cycle
  • Data collection and reporting
  • Earned value
  • Project control
  • Designing the control system
  • Scope creep and
