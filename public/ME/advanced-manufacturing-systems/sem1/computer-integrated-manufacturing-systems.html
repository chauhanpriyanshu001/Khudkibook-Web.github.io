Computer Integrated Manufacturing Systems Syllabus — Unit-wise Content

The official GTU curriculum for Computer Integrated Manufacturing Systems (subject code 2715006) as prescribed for AMS · Semester 1 divides the course into 4 units, covering 34 listed topics. The unit-wise breakdown below is read from the GTU syllabus document for this subject code, so you can check what the course actually covers before deciding which papers to attempt.

Download the official syllabus PDF (2715006)

Unit 1 Fundamentals of Manufacturing and Automation

8 lecture hours

  • Production operations and Automation in Production Systems
  • types of automation
  • Automation Principles & Strategies
  • Advance mfg. techniques like Digital manufacturing
  • RPT etc CIM Automation: Computer applications in Design and manufacture- Fundamentals of CAD, CAE, CAM
  • Introduction to CIM, Evolution
  • relationship between automation and CIM, reasons for automating
  • arguments for and against automation

Unit 2 High -Volume Production Systems

10 lecture hours

  • Automated flow lines
  • methods of work part transport
  • transfer mechanisms
  • buffer storage
  • analysis of flow lines without storage and with storage buffer
  • assembly systems
  • automated assembly system and its types
  • parts feeding devices
  • analysis of single stage and multistage assembly machines

Unit 3 Development and implementation of an FMS

12 lecture hours

  • Planning phase
  • System configuration
  • FMS layouts
  • FMS Project development steps
  • Project management
  • Equipment development
  • Host system development
  • Hardware & Software development
  • Automated Material Handling & Storage: Functions
  • Analysis of material handling equipment
  • Design of Conveyor & AGV systems
  • Development for total material handling system

Unit 4 Computer Aided Quality Control (CAQC)

12 lecture hours

  • QC inspection and testing
  • the computer in QC, Automated inspection principles and methods
  • sensor technologies for automated inspection – contact and non- contact types
  • computer aided testing
  • Integration of CAQC with CAD/CAM
