GTU Exam Preparation: 8 Practical Tips To Score Well In Theory & Viva

19 September 2026 6 min read Study Tips, Exam

GTU exams are different from what you probably did in school. The theory paper, the practical journal, and the viva all count separately — and most students lose easy marks simply because they did not understand how the marks are split. These eight tips come from years of talking to toppers and examiners. They work.

1. Start With Previous Year Papers, Not The Book

You cannot read a 400-page textbook in 15 days, and GTU often repeats question types year after year. Download free previous year question papers for your subject code first. You'll instantly see which units carry the most marks — study those units deeply and skip the trivia.

2. Learn The Paper Pattern Cold

GTU theory papers follow a fixed format — a mix of short-answer questions, unit-wise long questions, and usually a number of attempts with a choice. Know how many questions are compulsory, how much each question is worth, and how much time you can spend per question. This single habit stops the "I ran out of time" problem completely.

3. Write Answers The GTU Way

GTU marks headway: definitions and concepts first, then diagrams or derivations, then a short example. Examiners award step marks. So always:

  • Start every long answer with the correct definition.
  • Draw a labelled diagram/sketch where the question allows — it's free marks.
  • Show working for numericals, even if your final answer is wrong — step marks add up.
  • Underline key terms (unit name, formulas) so the examiner finds your answer instantly.

4. Build A Unit-Wise Formulary

For every subject, make a one-page cheat sheet: the 10–15 formulas/definitions per unit with their symbols. Revising one page per unit an hour before the exam fixes more marks than re-reading whole chapters. Keep them short — writing them out by hand is what makes them stick.

5. Don't Ignore The Easier Securities

Communication Skills, Environmental Science, Sports & Yoga and similar subjects carry the same credits but take one-tenth of the effort of technical subjects. A strong score here protects your SGPA even if one technical paper goes badly. Never treat them as "free" — but never over-prepare them either. Balanced effort wins the CGPA race.

6. Treat The Journal & Practicals As Marks In The Bank

Practical marks and the term work journal are usually awarded for completion. Submit every journal, every assignment, and every term work on time. Late or incomplete journals cost cheap marks that you literally cannot recover in the theory paper. Practical external exams reward students who can explain what they did — even an imperfectly executed experiment explained honestly scores better than a perfect one you can't describe.

7. Prepare For The Viva Like A Conversation

Viva examiners tend to start with simple, general questions and go deeper only if you invite it. Prepare the "big three" for every practical: What does it do?, How does it work?, and What are its applications / advantages and disadvantages?. If you don't know an answer, say "I know it relates to X, but I'm not fully sure" — examiners reward honesty and awareness over bluffing. Never stay silent.

8. Plan The Last 15 Days In Two Halves

  • Days 15–5: Cover the high-weightage units of every subject, one subject per day. Make your formulary sheets as you go.
  • Days 4–1: Solve two previous year papers per subject under real time conditions. Mark yourself harshly. Fix every repeated mistake.

Sleep 7+ hours. A tired brain loses far more marks in the exam hall than any night of panic studying ever gains.
