GTU Electrical Engineering: Subjects, Skills & Career Scope

7 October 2026 7 min read Electrical Engineering, Branch Guides, Career, Subjects

Electrical Engineering is the branch behind the power grid, the machines in a factory, and the electronics in your phone's charger. It is a safety-critical field with steady demand — every industry that uses electricity needs people who understand it. Here is what the GTU course covers and how to turn it into a career.

Exact subjects and credits vary by scheme and admission year. Use this as an overview, then check your own codes on the Electrical Engineering page.

What you study

  • Electrical circuits — the foundation: current, voltage, networks, AC/DC.
  • Electrical machines — motors, generators and transformers.
  • Power systems — generation, transmission, distribution and protection.
  • Measurements & instrumentation — measuring electrical quantities accurately.
  • Power electronics — controlling power with semiconductor devices.
  • Control systems — feedback and automation.
  • Switchgear & protection — keeping systems safe from faults.
  • Electrical drawing, estimation & wiring — the practical side of the trade.

Skills that get you hired

  1. Safety first — electrical work is dangerous; understanding safety rules is non-negotiable.
  2. Reading electrical drawings — schematics and wiring diagrams.
  3. Wiring and testing — hands-on competence with panels, meters and circuits.
  4. Motor and transformer knowledge — the workhorses of industry.
  5. Basic PLC/automation exposure — modern plants run on automation; even basics set you apart.
  6. Estimation — costing and planning electrical installations.

How to make the most of it

  • Master the fundamentals of circuits — everything later builds on them.
  • Get hands-on with real equipment. Lab time matters as much as theory here.
  • Learn to draw and read schematics fluently.
  • Respect safety procedures — they are part of the profession, not fine print.
  • Explore automation early. Even a little PLC knowledge opens plant-floor roles.
