Fundamentals of Digital Design Syllabus — Unit-wise Content

The official GTU curriculum for Fundamentals of Digital Design (subject code 2130306) as prescribed for Biomed · Semester 3 divides the course into 7 units, covering 53 listed topics. The unit-wise breakdown below is read from the GTU syllabus document for this subject code, so you can check what the course actually covers before deciding which papers to attempt.

Download the official syllabus PDF (2130306)

Unit 1 Introduction to Digital Design & Digital Logic

4 lecture hours

  • Difference between analogue & digital systems
  • Radix number systems
  • Conversion among radices
  • Numeric complements representation
  • Signed fixed point number representation
  • Floating point representation
  • Binary codes representation
  • Character representation

Unit 2 Binary Logic & Boolean Algebra

8 lecture hours

  • Logic gates with truth tables (AND, OR, NOT, NAND, NOR, EX- OR, EX-NOR)
  • NAND & NOR implementations
  • Introduction to Integrated Circuits
  • Introduction to logic families (DL, RTL, DTL, TTL, ECL, CMOS)
  • Definition of fan-in
  • switching times
  • noise margin
  • Postulate & laws of boolean algebra
  • De Morgan's Theorem
  • Simplification of Expressions (SOP, POS, Minterm
  • Gate level minimization using K-maps (upto 3-4 variables) or Tabulation method (Quine McCluskey method)
  • Don’t care conditions
  • Two-level/Multi-level circuit optimization & design

Unit 3 Designing Combinational Circuits

8 lecture hours

  • Use of 1’s & 2’s complement in adders/subtractors
  • Steps for the design of combinational circuits
  • Design of adders (Half
  • & Carry look ahead)
  • Design of subtractors (Half
  • Design of binary subtractor using adders
  • Design of encoders & decoders
  • Design of multiplexers & demultiplexers
  • Design of code converters & magnitude comparator

Unit 4 Designing of programmable logic

8 lecture hours

  • Introduction to programmable circuits
  • Circuit implementation using PLDs (ROM, PLA, PAL)
  • Realization of Switching Functions Using PROM
  • PAL and PLA, Determine the equation for the logic function being implemented for a PAL logic diagram
  • Draw the fuse map needed to implement a simple function with a PAL, Use a PAL data sheet to determine the device needed to implement a specified logic function

Unit 5 Designing Sequential Circuits

8 lecture hours

  • Introduction to latches & flip-flops
  • Design of Sequential Modules – RS, JK, D, T flip-flops
  • Moore & Mealy model of sequential circuit
  • Steps for the design of sequential circuits
  • Sequential circuit design: unused states and verification (timing issues in Mealy machines)
  • State machine diagrams and constraints
  • State diagrams of the four types of flip-flops
  • Flip-flop applications – Clock generation
  • Practical Considerations (debouncing
  • delay and timing

Unit 6 A/D & D/A converters

4 lecture hours

  • Different types of D/A converter
  • D/A accuracy and resolution
  • Different types of A/D converter
  • A/D accuracy and resolution

Unit 7 Simulation and Synthesis with a Hardware Description Language

4 lecture hours

  • Introduction to Hardware Description Language (HDL) (VHDL
  • Introduction to simulation & synthesis software (Xilinx
  • Max+plus II)
  • Introduction to FPGA & CPLD
