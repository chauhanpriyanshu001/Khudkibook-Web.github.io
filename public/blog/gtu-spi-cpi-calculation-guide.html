How GTU Calculates SPI and CPI (With A Full Worked Example)

8 October 2026 6 min read SPI, CPI, GTU Result, Grading

GTU results are not an average of your marks — they are a credit-weighted average of grade points. Once you understand that one sentence, SPI and CPI stop being mysterious, and you can predict your own result before it is declared. Here is the whole calculation, start to finish.

Step 1: Know the 10-point grade scale

Each subject grade carries a grade point on a 10-point scale. The common CBCS scale is:

GradeGrade pointRoughly
O (Outstanding)1090%+
A+980–89%
A870–79%
B+760–69%
B650–59%
C545–49%
D440–44%
F0Fail

Always confirm the exact bands for your programme and admission year in your scheme's regulations — the pass mark and some bands differ slightly across programmes.

Step 2: SPI = credit-weighted average

SPI = Σ(grade point × subject credits) ÷ Σ(subject credits) for one semester. Notice that a 5-credit subject pulls your SPI harder than a 3-credit one — credits are the weights.

Worked example — one semester

SubjectCreditsGradeGrade pointCredits × point
Mathematics5A+945
Physics4A832
Programming4O1040
Communication Skills3B+721
Workshop2A816
Total18154

SPI = 154 ÷ 18 = 8.56. Convert to percentage with the official formula: (8.56 − 0.5) × 10 = 80.6%.

Step 3: CPI across all semesters

CPI is the same credit-weighted average, but gathered over every semester you have completed. The cleanest way to think about it: total all (grade point × credits) from every subject you have passed so far, then divide by the total credits.

A faster shortcut once you have each semester's SPI: weight each SPI by that semester's credits and average. A semester with more credits moves your CPI more.

Why this matters while you study

  • High-credit subjects are worth more. A strong grade in a 5-credit core subject lifts your CPI more than the same grade in a 2-credit elective. Spend your revision time accordingly.
  • Backlogs hurt twice. A failed subject contributes 0 points until cleared, and it delays the credits that raise your CPI.
  • Consistency beats a single spike. Because CPI accumulates, one brilliant semester cannot carry the degree on its own — but steady semesters compound.
