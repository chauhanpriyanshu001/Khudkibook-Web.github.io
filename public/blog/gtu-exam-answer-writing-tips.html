How To Write GTU Exam Answers To Score More Marks

16 October 2026 6 min read GTU Exam, Study Tips, Answer Writing

Every exam season, students who understood the subject lose marks to students who present better. It is unfair, and it is also fixable. GTU answer scripts are marked point-wise against a scheme, so an examiner who can find your points can award them quickly. Structure your answer so the examiner never has to hunt.

Start with the marks, not the topic

Read the whole paper first and note how many marks each question carries. A 7-mark question expects depth; a 3-mark one expects a crisp definition and an example. Dividing your time in proportion to marks is the single highest-return habit in the exam hall.

MarksWhat earns them
2–3A precise definition or short list. One or two lines.
4–5A short explanation plus one example or a small labelled diagram.
7–8A structured answer: definition, explanation in points, diagram, and an example.

Structure every long answer the same way

  1. Definition / statement — one line stating what the concept is.
  2. Numbered points — the examiner awards marks per point, so write in points rather than paragraphs.
  3. Diagram — if the topic can be drawn, draw it and label it. A neat labelled diagram often carries marks on its own.
  4. Example / application — a one-line example shows understanding, not memory.
  5. Conclusion — a single line that ties it together, where the question asks for one.

Underline keywords and headings. A script that looks organised is easier to mark, and an examiner under time pressure rewards clarity.

Match the outcome verb

The syllabus tells you the depth expected through its outcome verbs:

  • Define / List / State — do not write a paragraph; give the exact definition or list.
  • Explain / Describe — points plus an example.
  • Derive / Solve / Calculate — full step-by-step working with formulas, substitutions and units.
  • Compare / Differentiate — a two-column table, point by point.
  • Classify / Draw — a labelled diagram or a clearly categorised tree.

Never leave a question blank

Attempt every question you have read, even partially. Marking is usually point-wise, so three correct points on a 7-mark question are worth more than a blank page. Write the definition, a formula, or the diagram you remember — something almost always earns marks.

Use the last ten minutes to check

  1. Did you attempt the required number of questions, and in the right sections?
  2. Are question numbers written clearly against every answer?
  3. Are units present in every numerical answer?
  4. Any page left with space that could hold a diagram or a missed point?
Practise answer writing, not just reading. Take a past paper and write full answers under a timer — presentation improves only with reps.

Before the exam, know the format

Answer presentation is easier when you already know the paper pattern — how many questions, how many to attempt, and how marks are split. Reading several previous-year papers for your subject code tells you all of that in an hour.
