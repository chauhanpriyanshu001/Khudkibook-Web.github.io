Power Plant Automation Syllabus — Unit-wise Content

The official GTU curriculum for Power Plant Automation (subject code 4364103) as prescribed for Nano · Semester 6 divides the course into 5 units, covering 25 listed topics. The unit-wise breakdown below is read from the GTU syllabus document for this subject code, so you can check what the course actually covers before deciding which papers to attempt.

Download the official syllabus PDF (4364103)

Unit 1 Introduction to Power Plants

  • 1.1. Overview of Power Plants 1.1a Classification of power plants: Thermal, Nuclear, Hydroelectric, and Renewable Energy-based. 1.1b Importance and role of power plants in the energy sector. 1.1c Discuss the role of renewable energy power plants in modern energy systems.
  • 1.2. Layouts of Power Plants 1.2a Comparative layouts of nuclear, hydroelectric, and renewable energy power plants. 1.2b General layout of thermal power plants. 1.2c Energy flow and conversion processes in thermal power plants using block diagram.
  • 1.3. Compare different types of power plants and their suitability based on energy sources.
  • 1.4. Thermal Power Plant 1.4a Components: Boiler, Turbine, Generator, Condenser, Cooling Tower. 1.4b Rankine Cycle in thermal power plants. 1.4c Advantages and limitations of thermal power plants. Power Plant Automation Course Code: 4364103

Unit 2 Major Equipment of Thermal Power Plants

  • 2.1. Boilers and Their Types 2.1a Subcritical, Supercritical, and Ultra-supercritical boilers. 2.1b Boiler design, operation, and maintenance.
  • 2.2. Steam Turbines / Gas Turbine 2.2a Types: Impulse and Reaction turbines. 2.2b Role of turbines in energy conversion. 2.2c Gas Turbine Operations refer (Mark VI) control GE
  • 2.3. Condensers and Cooling Systems 2.3a Surface and jet condensers. 2.3b Types of cooling towers: Natural draft, forced draft, and induced draft.
  • 2.4. Auxiliary Systems 2.4a Coal handling and feeding systems. 2.4b Ash handling systems. 2.4c Water treatment and feedwater systems. 2.4d DM Tank Operations

Unit 3 Control Loops in Power Plants

  • 3.1. Introduction to Control Loops 3.1a Concept of closed-loop control. 3.1b Importance of control loops in power plant operation.
  • 3.2. Process Diagrams and Interpretation 3.2a Understanding and interpreting P&ID diagrams. 3.2b Symbols and notations used in control loops
  • 3.3. Key Control Loops in Thermal Power Plants 3.3a Deaerator control loop. 3.3b Feedwater control loop. 3.3c Superheater and reheater control loops. 3.3d Economizer control loop. 3.3e Turbine speed and load control Power Plant Automation Course Code: 4364103
  • 3.4. Steam Drum Level Controller : Explain Two element – Three element Boiler Level Control Loop
  • 3.5. Discuss the importance of feedwater control in maintaining boiler efficiency.
  • 3.6. With the help of a P&ID, explain the reheater control loop in a thermal power plant.
  • 3.7. Define the economizer control loop and explain how it optimizes fuel usage.

Unit 4 Instrumenta tion Systems in Power Plants 4.a Understand the latest trends and technologies in power plant instrumentation.

  • 4.1. Discuss overview of Instrumentation in Power Plants 4.1a. Role of sensors, transmitters, and controllers. 4.1b. Importance of real-time monitoring systems.
  • 4.2. Latest Trends in Instrumentation 4.2a. Distributed Control Systems (DCS). 4.2b. Emergency Shutdown System (ESD) – Cause and Effect Matrix with Generator related tripping controls 4.2c.Internet of Things (IoT) in power plants.
  • 4.3. Advanced Measurement Systems 4.3a Flue gas analyzers and emission monitoring systems. 4.3b Vibration monitoring for turbines. 4.3c Temperature, pressure, and flow measurement technologies.
  • 4.4. Instrumentation Systems by Industry Leaders 4.4a Overview of ABB, GE, and Siemens technologies. 4.4b Case examples of implementation in power plants. 4.4c Compare the features of ABB, GE, and Siemens instrumentation systems. Power Plant Automation Course Code: 4364103
  • 4.5. Explain the role of IoT in modern power plant operations.

  • 5.1. Present a case study on a thermal power plant’s automation system and discuss its success factors.
  • 5.2. Analyse a failure incident in power plant automation and suggest improvements. 5.2a Analysis of incidents caused by instrumentation or automation failures. 5.2b Root cause analysis and corrective measures.
  • 5.3. A case study: instrumentation upgrades significantly improved power plant performance. 5.3a Case studies on performance improvement through upgraded instrumentation. 5.3b Reduction in emissions and compliance with environmental standards
  • 5.4. Explain the challenges faced in integrating renewable energy systems into conventional power plants.
