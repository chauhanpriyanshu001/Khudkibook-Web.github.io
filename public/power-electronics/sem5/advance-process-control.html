Advance Process Control Syllabus — Unit-wise Content

The official GTU curriculum for Advance Process Control (subject code DI05017031) as prescribed for PE · Semester 5 divides the course into 5 units, covering 70 listed topics. The unit-wise breakdown below is read from the GTU syllabus document for this subject code, so you can check what the course actually covers before deciding which papers to attempt.

Download the official syllabus PDF (DI05017031)

Unit 1 Introduction to Process Control

  • 1.1. Introduction  Brief review of process and process control concepts  Role of process control in modern industries  Applications of process control in industries (chemical, thermal, manufacturing, automation systems)
  • 1.2. Types of Processes  Types of industrial processes (overview only)  Characteristics of industrial processes (non- linearity, dead time, disturbances)  Examples of industrial processes and control challenges
  • 1.3. Industrial Challenges in Process Control  Challenges in controlling real industrial processes  Effect of disturbances and uncertainties  Performance requirements (accuracy, stability, robustness)
  • 1.4. Process Variables  Process variables in industrial context  Interaction of controlled, manipulated, and disturbance variables
  • 1.5. Control System Configuration  Industrial control system architecture (overview)  Limitations of open-loop and simple feedback systems in industries  Need for improved control strategies
  • 1.6. Control Objectives in Industrial Systems  Servo and regulatory control (brief overview)

  • 1.7. Control Strategies  Feedback control and its industrial significance  Feedforward control for disturbance rejection
  • 1.8. Process Control System Design  Steps in process control system design (overview)  Selection of control strategy based on process requirements  Role of simulation and testing in control design

Unit 2 Closed-loop Response

  • 2.1. Closed-loop Control System (Overview)  Brief review of closed-loop control system  Practical understanding of closed-loop behavior in industrial control systems
  • 2.2. Controllers in Industrial Systems  Role of P, PI, PD, PID controllers in process industries  Effect of controllers on system performance  Comparison based on industrial applications
  • 2.3. System Response Analysis  Interpretation of system response for standard inputs (step, ramp, etc.)  Time response parameters (rise time, settling time, overshoot)
  • 2.4. Integral Windup and Anti-windup  Concept of integral windup  Methods of anti-windup
  • 2.5. Controller Tuning Methods  Process Reaction Curve method  Ziegler–Nichols tuning method
  • 2.6. Performance Evaluation Criteria  Integral Absolute Error (IAE)  Integral Square Error (ISE)  Integral Time Absolute Error (ITAE)

Unit 3 Digital Controllers

  • 3.1. Introduction to Digital Control Systems  Concept of digital control  Comparison of analog and digital control systems  Advantages of digital control systems
  • 3.2. Direct Digital Control (DDC) System  Definition of Direct Digital Control (DDC)  Basic components of DDC system

  • 3.3. Communication and Operation of DDC  Signal flow in DDC system  Data acquisition and control action  Role of sensors, actuators, and controllers in DDC
  • 3.4. Benefits and Limitations of DDC  Advantages of DDC systems  Limitations of DDC systems
  • 3.5. Digital Controller Realization  Concept of digital controller realization  Basic implementation of digital controllers using computer/microprocessor
  • 3.6. Discrete Domain Analysis  Concept of discrete-time signals  Sampling and quantization  Difference between continuous-time and discrete- time systems  Basic idea of discrete domain representation
  • 3.7. Applications of DDC  DDC of air heater  DDC of DC motor speed control
  • 3.8. Future Trends in Digital Control  IoT integration in control systems  Machine learning in control systems  Advanced analytics in control

Unit 4 Advanced Control Strategies

  • 4.1. Cascade Control  Concept and features of cascade control  Block diagram of cascade control system  Advantages of cascade control  Applications: Jacketed CSTR, Boiler Drum Level Control
  • 4.2. Feedforward Control  Detailed concept of feedforward control  Feedforward control configuration  Advantages and limitations of feedforward control  Applications: Distillation Column, Heat Exchanger, Drum Boiler
  • 4.3. Feedforward–Feedback Control  Concept of combined feedforward–feedback control

  • 4.4. Ratio Control  Concept of ratio control  Basic configuration of ratio control system  Applications of ratio control
  • 4.5. Selective Control  Concept of selective (override) control  Types of selective control  Applications: o Protection of boiler drum o Protection of steam distribution system o Prevention of flooding in distillation column  Split-range control
  • 4.6. Adaptive Control  Concept of adaptive control  Types of adaptive control: o Programmed adaptive control o Self-adaptive control o Model reference adaptive control o Self-tuning regulator  Adaptive control configuration

Unit 5 Introduction to Neural-Fuzzy System

  • 5.2. Types of ANN (Overview)  Types of neural networks (basic idea)  Feedforward neural network  Feedback (recurrent) neural network
  • 5.3. Applications of ANN  Applications of ANN in control systems  Industrial applications of ANN
  • 5.4. Introduction to Fuzzy Logic  Concept of fuzzy logic  Crisp logic vs fuzzy logic
  • 5.5. Basic Fuzzy Logic System  Basic structure of fuzzy logic system  Fuzzification and defuzzification (concept only)
  • 5.6. Comparison and Applications  Basic comparison of ANN and fuzzy logic
