## 5.1. Waves, wave motion, and types of waves: longitudinal and transverse waves

### Introduction to Waves
A **wave** is a disturbance that travels through a medium, carrying energy from one point to another without the transfer of matter. Waves can be classified based on their direction of vibration relative to the direction of propagation. The two main types of waves are **longitudinal waves** and **transverse waves**.

### Types of Waves: Longitudinal and Transverse Waves

#### Longitudinal Waves
- **Definition**: In a **longitudinal wave**, the particles of the medium vibrate back and forth parallel to the direction of wave propagation. Examples include sound waves in air and seismic P-waves.
- **Examples**: 
  - **Sound Waves**: In air, sound waves are longitudinal. The particles of air vibrate back and forth, causing the pressure to vary in the air.
  - **Seismic P-waves**: These are also longitudinal. The particles of the earth move back and forth in the direction of wave propagation.

#### Transverse Waves
- **Definition**: In a **transverse wave**, the particles of the medium vibrate perpendicular to the direction of wave propagation. Examples include water waves and electromagnetic waves.
- **Examples**: 
  - **Water Waves**: When a stone is dropped into a calm pond, ripples are created. The water particles move up and down, while the wave moves outward.
  - **Electromagnetic Waves**: These waves include light, microwaves, and radio waves. The electric and magnetic fields oscillate perpendicular to the direction of wave propagation.

### Example:
> **Example:** 
> Consider a longitudinal wave traveling through a string. If the wave is propagating from left to right, the particles of the string will vibrate back and forth along the same direction, parallel to the wave's direction of travel.

## 5.2. Frequency, periodic time, amplitude, wave length and wave velocity and their relationship

### Definitions and Key Terms
- **Frequency (f)**: The number of cycles or waves that pass a given point in a medium per second. It is measured in **Hertz (Hz)**.
- **Periodic Time (T)**: The time taken for one complete cycle or wave to pass a given point. It is the inverse of frequency, i.e., T = 1 f.
- **Amplitude (A)**: The maximum displacement of a particle from its equilibrium position. It is a measure of the energy of the wave.
- **Wavelength (λ)**: The distance between two consecutive points in phase in a wave. It is the spatial period of the wave.
- **Wave Velocity (v)**: The speed at which a wave propagates through a medium. It is the distance traveled per unit time.

### Relationship Between Frequency, Periodic Time, Amplitude, Wavelength, and Wave Velocity
- **Wave Velocity (v)**: It is the product of frequency (f) and wavelength (λ). Therefore, v = f · λ.
- **Frequency and Periodic Time Relationship**: f = 1 T or T = 1 f.

### Example:
> **Example:** 
> A wave has a frequency of 5 Hz and a wavelength of 2 meters. Calculate the wave velocity.
> 
> Given:
> - Frequency, f = 5 Hz
> - Wavelength, λ = 2 m
> 
> Using the relationship v = f · λ:
> 
> [
> v = 5 \, \text{Hz} \times 2 \, \text{m} = 10 \, \text{m/s}
> ]
> 
> Therefore, the wave velocity is 10 meters per second.
