2.1.1. Nouns

Noun: A noun is a word that refers to a person, place, thing, or idea. Nouns can be singular or plural, and they can be common or proper.

  • Common Nouns: Refer to general things, such as "book," "city," or "dog."
  • Proper Nouns: Refer to specific people, places, or things, such as "London," "John," or "Sunday."

Example:

  • Common Noun: She reads a book.
  • Proper Noun: She reads a Harry Potter book.
Example
Identify the noun in the sentence: "The teacher gave the book to John."
> Answer: The nouns are "teacher," "book," and "John."

2.1.2. Pronouns

Pronoun: A pronoun is a word used instead of a noun to avoid repetition. Common pronouns include "I," "you," "he," "she," "it," "we," "they," "me," "him," "her," "us," "them," "this," "that," "these," and "those."

  • Personal Pronouns: First person singular (I, me), first person plural (we, us), second person singular and plural (you), third person singular (he, she, it) and plural (they, them).
  • Possessive Pronouns: Mine, yours, his, hers, its, ours, theirs.
  • Reflexive Pronouns: Myself, yourself, himself, herself, itself, ourselves, yourselves, themselves.

Example:

  • Personal Pronoun: I read a book. You read a book.
  • Possessive Pronoun: This is mine.
  • Reflexive Pronoun: She hurt herself.
Example
Choose the correct pronoun to complete the sentence: "The book belongs ___."
> Answer: The book belongs to me.

2.1.3. Verbs

Verb: A verb is a word that expresses an action, occurrence, or state of being. Verbs are often used with subjects and objects to form complete sentences.

  • Finite Verbs: Can be conjugated and agree with the subject in number and person. Examples: "read," "am reading," "read."
  • Non-finite Verbs: Cannot be conjugated and are not used with a subject. Examples: "reading," "to read," "read."

Example:

  • Finite Verb: She reads a book.
  • Non-finite Verb: To read a book is good.
Example
Identify the verb in the sentence: "She reads a book in the park."
> Answer: Reads.

2.1.4. Adjectives

Adjective: An adjective is a word that modifies a noun or pronoun, giving more information about it.

  • Descriptive Adjectives: Describe the noun directly, such as "big," "red," "happy."
  • Possessive Adjectives: Indicate ownership, such as "my," "your," "his," "her."

Example:

  • Descriptive Adjective: The red car is fast.
  • Possessive Adjective: This is my book.
Example
Choose the correct adjective to complete the sentence: "The ___ book is in the library."
> Answer: The red book is in the library.

2.1.5. Adverbs

Adverb: An adverb is a word that modifies a verb, adjective, or other adverb, giving more information about the action, quality, or manner.

  • Manner: How something is done, such as "quickly," "carefully."
  • Time: When something happens, such as "now," "often."
  • Place: Where something happens, such as "here," "there."
  • Degree: How much something happens, such as "very," "extremely."

Example:

  • Manner Adverb: She reads quickly.
  • Time Adverb: He often comes early.
  • Place Adverb: The meeting is here.
Example
Choose the correct adverb to complete the sentence: "He reads ___."
> Answer: He reads quickly.

2.1.6. Interjections

Interjection: An interjection is a word that expresses a sudden feeling or emotion, such as "oh," "ah," "wow," "ouch," "hello," "goodbye."

Example:

  • Interjection: Oh, no! The book is lost.

2.1.7. Prepositions

Preposition: A preposition shows the relationship between a noun or pronoun and other words in a sentence. Common prepositions include "in," "into," "on," "at," "for," "since," "between," "among," "to," "towards."

Example:

  • In: She is in the garden.
  • Into: She went into the house.
  • On: He put the book on the table.
  • At: He is at the park.
  • For: She is waiting for her friend.
  • Since: Since you asked, I will help.
  • Between: There is a fence between the houses.
  • Among: They are among the trees.
  • To: He gave the book to her.
  • Towards: He ran towards the goal.
Example
Choose the correct preposition to complete the sentence: "He is ___ the park."
> Answer: He is at the park.

2.1.8. Connectors

Connectors: Connectors, also known as conjunctions, are words that join words, phrases, clauses, or sentences.

  • Conditional Connectors: If, unless, otherwise.
  • Cause and Effect Connectors: Because, therefore.
  • Relative Connectors: Who, which, where, when, why.

Example:

  • Conditional Connector: If you come, you will have fun.
  • Cause and Effect Connector: Because it is raining, we will stay inside.
  • Relative Connector: The book, which is mine, is in the library.
Example

2.2.1.1. Simple Present Tense

The Simple Present Tense is used to describe actions that are habitual, general truths, or ongoing states. It is formed by using the base form of the verb for most subjects, and -s or -es for third-person singular subjects.

  • Subject + Verb (Base form or -s/-es)
  • Example: I write a letter every day.
  • Example: He reads a book every night.

2.2.1.2. Present Continuous Tense

The Present Continuous Tense is used to describe actions that are happening now or are in progress. It is formed by using the present tense of the verb "to be" followed by the present participle (verb + -ing).

  • Subject + am/is/are + verb (base form) + -ing
  • Example: She is writing a letter now.
  • Example: They are reading a book.

2.2.1.3. Present Perfect Tense

The Present Perfect Tense is used to describe actions that have happened at an indefinite time in the past but are relevant to the present. It is formed by using "have" or "has" followed by the past participle (verb + -ed).

  • Subject + have/has + verb (past participle)
  • Example: I have written a letter.
  • Example: They have read the book.

2.2.1.4. Present Perfect Continuous Tense

The Present Perfect Continuous Tense is used to describe actions that started in the past and have continued up to the present. It is formed by using "have" or "has" followed by the present participle (verb + -ing).

  • Subject + have/has + been + verb (base form) + -ing
  • Example: She has been writing a letter for an hour.
  • Example: They have been reading the book since morning.

2.2.3. Future Tense (Simple)

Introduction

The future tense is used to describe actions or events that will happen in the future. It is essential for expressing intentions, predictions, and plans. The simple future tense is formed using the auxiliary verb will followed by the base form of the main verb.

Formation of Future Tense

  • Positive Statements:
  • Subject + will + base form of the verb + object.
  • Example: She will go to the library tomorrow.
  • Negative Statements:
  • Subject + will + not + base form of the verb + object.
  • Example: They will not go to the park today.
  • Questions:
  • Will + subject + base form of the verb + object?
  • Example: Will you come to the meeting tomorrow?

Usage in Different Contexts

  • Intention or Plan:
  • The future tense can be used to express intentions or plans.
  • Example: I will finish my project by next week.
  • Prediction:
  • It can also be used to make predictions.
  • Example: It will rain tomorrow.
  • Promise or Offer:
  • The future tense can be used to make promises or offers.
  • Example: I will help you with your homework.

Example

Example

- Positive Statement: She will go to the library tomorrow.
- Negative Statement: They will not go to the park today.
- Question: Will you come to the meeting tomorrow?

2.4. Subject- Verb Agreement

Subject-verb agreement is a fundamental aspect of English grammar. It involves ensuring that the subject and verb of a sentence agree in number (singular or plural). This agreement is crucial for clear and correct communication.

2.4.1. Defining Subject and Verb

  • Subject: The subject is the person, place, thing, or idea that is doing or being something in the sentence. It is the part of the sentence that performs the action or is being described.
  • Verb: The verb is the action word or state of being in the sentence. It indicates what the subject is doing or being.

2.4.2. Singular and Plural Subjects

  • Singular Subject: A single person, place, thing, or idea. Examples: "The cat," "John," "The book."
  • Plural Subject: More than one person, place, thing, or idea. Examples: "The cats," "John and Mary," "The books."

2.4.3. Regular and Irregular Verbs

  • Regular Verb: Verbs that form their past tense and past participle by adding -ed to the base form. Examples: "walked," "talked."
  • Irregular Verb: Verbs that do not follow the standard -ed rule. Examples: "walked," "talked."

2.4.4. Subject-Verb Agreement Rules

  1. Singular Subject with Singular Verb:
  • Example: "The dog barks."
  1. Plural Subject with Plural Verb:
  • Example: "The dogs bark."
  1. Singular Subject with Collective Noun and Plural Verb:
  • Example: "The committee are discussing the issue."
  1. Singular Subject with Non-Singular Verb:
  • Example: "The news is on the television."
  1. Compound Subject with Plural Verb:
  • Example: "John and Mary are going to the park."

2.4.5. Subject-Verb Agreement with Prepositional Phrases

  • A prepositional phrase can come between the subject and the verb, but the subject remains the same.
  • Example: "The book on the table is new."
  • Example: "The students in the class are from different countries."

2.4.6. Subject-Verb Agreement with Indefinite Pronouns

  • Indefinite Pronouns: Words like "each," "everyone," "anyone," "someone," "everybody," "nobody," "somebody," "anybody."
  • Example: "Each student has completed the assignment."
  • Example: "Everyone in the room is happy."

2.4.7. Subject-Verb Agreement with Collective Nouns

  • Collective Noun: A noun that refers to a group of people or things but can be treated as a singular or plural subject depending on the context.
  • Example: "The team is ready to play."
  • Example: "The team are divided on the issue."

2.4.8. Subject-Verb Agreement with Indefinite Quantifiers

  • Indefinite Quantifiers: Words like "some," "many," "few," "much."
  • Example: "There is much to be done."
  • Example: "There are many books on the shelf."

2.4.9. Subject-Verb Agreement with Compound Subjects

  • Compound Subject: A subject that consists of two or more subjects joined by "and," "or," "nor," "but," "for," "so," "yet."
  • Example: "John and Mary are going to the park."
  • Example: "Neither the students nor the teacher is late."
