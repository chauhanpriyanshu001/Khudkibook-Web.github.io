A 30-Day GTU Exam Preparation Plan That Actually Fits

14 October 2026 6 min read Study Plan, GTU Exam, Study Tips, Time Management

Most students study for a GTU exam by starting with the first unit and reading until time runs out. That fails because the paper does not weigh every unit equally. This plan spends your thirty days in proportion to what the paper actually asks — and it builds in the two things most revision misses: solving past papers under time, and revising what you got wrong.

Adjust the block lengths to the number of subjects and papers you personally have. The order below matters more than the exact number of days.

Before day one: spend two hours on setup

  1. List your subjects and their credits. High-credit subjects deserve more days.
  2. For each subject, list units with their marks weightage from the syllabus — every subject page on Khudkibook shows this.
  3. Collect 4–5 years of previous papers for each subject code.
  4. Build a simple grid: subject × unit, marked with the weightage. This becomes your control board for the month.

Weeks 1–2: cover the heavy units (first pass)

Start with the highest-credit subject and, within it, the highest-weight units. The goal of this first pass is understanding, not memorising: read the topic, write a one-page summary in your own words, and move on. Do not fall into polishing one unit for days.

  • Split each day into two or three blocks of about 90 minutes, with a short break between. Deep work in blocks beats hours of distracted reading.
  • Rotate subjects so no subject goes more than a few days untouched.
  • Mark every unit as you finish it: green (understood), amber (shaky), red (not done).

Week 3: switch to past papers

This is the week most students get wrong by continuing to read. Reading feels productive; solving papers is what actually raises marks. For every subject, start solving full papers under a timer.

  1. Solve one full paper per subject as a timed mock, in exam conditions.
  2. Mark yourself honestly against the syllabus and model answers/notes.
  3. List every question you could not answer and add those topics back to the control board as red/amber.
  4. Notice the pattern — which units and question types repeat. GTU papers are far more predictable than they feel.

Week 4: fix the reds and revise

  • Attack only the red and amber topics from your paper practice. This is targeted revision, the highest-return study there is.
  • Redo the derivation and numerical problems you missed — they repeat.
  • Compress each subject to a few pages of formulas, definitions and diagrams for the final day.
  • Solve one more timed paper per subject in the last three days and check that your red list is shrinking.

The night before

Do not start a new topic. Read your compressed sheets, revise definitions and diagrams, and sleep. A rested brain recalls the units you already studied far better than a tired brain that crammed a new one. Write the exam with the answer structure from our answer-writing guide in mind.

Everything in this plan starts with your subject's unit-wise weightage and past papers — both free on Khudkibook.
