GTU Summer vs Winter Exam: What Is The Difference & Which Attempt Matters

6 October 2026 5 min read GTU Exam, Winter, Summer, Backlog

New GTU students are often confused by "Summer" and "Winter" exams — the names sound like weather but they actually describe two exam sessions and two sets of semesters. Getting this straight early saves a lot of guesswork when exam forms open.

The two sessions

SessionUsually heldTypically covers
WinterNovember–DecemberOdd semesters (1, 3, 5, 7, and equivalent)
SummerApril–MayEven semesters (2, 4, 6, 8, and equivalent)

Your academic calendar is the authority for the exact dates each year — GTU publishes it and updates it through official circulars. The site's GTU Notices page tracks those circulars as they are released.

Why the "wrong" session comes up for backlogs

If you have to clear a subject, you cannot always appear for it in your next regular session, because that session sits a different set of semesters. This is the single biggest source of confusion. The practical rule:

  • Regular (first attempt): you appear in the session that matches the semester you are studying — Winter for odd, Summer for even.
  • Backlog / re-attempt: GTU ordinarily schedules a re-attempt for a failed subject in the same session type as the subject belongs to, in the following year's cycle. So an odd-semester backlog is usually re-attempted in a later Winter session, and an even-semester backlog in a later Summer session.

Always read the specific circular for your programme, because the university can and does add remedial sessions or adjust rules from time to time.

After the result: recheck, remand and remedy

If a paper does not go your way, GTU gives a few formal routes, each with a window and a fee:

  • Recheck — a clerical verification of total marks (no re-evaluation of answers).
  • Remand — a re-assessment of the answer book by another examiner.
  • Re-attempt — sitting the paper again in a later session.

Announcements for these are published together with the result. If a deadline matters to you, note it the day the result is out — these windows are short.

How to plan

  • Match your prep to your session. If you are in even semesters, your big exam is Summer; plan your revision around the academic calendar, not the calendar year.
  • Treat backlogs as a scheduling problem. Find out which session your backlog subject belongs to and set a reminder for that exam form — missing the form costs you a full cycle.
  • Practise with the right papers. A session's paper follows that session's pattern; solve enough of both so nothing about the format surprises you.
