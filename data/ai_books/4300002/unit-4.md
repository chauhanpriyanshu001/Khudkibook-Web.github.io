## 4.1. Email Writing (Business) Format and Sample Enquiry, Orders and Complaints Examples for Practice

### 4.1.1. Business Email Format
Writing effective business emails is crucial for professional communication. The standard format for a business email includes the following sections:

- **Header:** Contains the sender's and recipient's email addresses, date, and subject line.
- **Greeting:** A formal greeting, typically "Dear [Recipient's Name]," or "To Whom It May Concern," or "Dear Sir/Madam."
- **Body:** The main content of the email, divided into paragraphs.
- **Closing:** A formal sign-off, such as "Sincerely," "Best regards," "Yours faithfully," or "Thank you."
- **Signature:** Personal and professional details like name, position, and contact information.

### 4.1.2. Sample Enquiry Email
Let’s create a sample email to inquire about a product from a supplier.

> **Example:**
>
> **Header:**
>
> From: [Your Email Address]  
> To: [Supplier's Email Address]  
> Date: 10th April 2023  
> Subject: Inquiry about Product XYZ
>
> **Greeting:**
>
> Dear Mr. Patel,
>
> **Body:**
>
> I hope this email finds you well. I am writing to inquire about your company's product, Product XYZ, which I recently came across on your website. Could you please provide me with the following details:
>
> - The current stock availability.
> - The wholesale price per unit.
> - The minimum order quantity.
> - The lead time for delivery.
>
> Additionally, I would like to know if there are any ongoing discounts or promotions that could be applied to this order.
>
> **Closing:**
>
> I look forward to your prompt response. Thank you for your time and assistance.
>
> **Signature:**
>
> Sincerely,  
> [Your Name]  
> [Your Position]  
> [Your Company Name]  
> [Your Contact Number]  
> [Your Email Address]

## 4.2. Letter Writing

### Types of Letters

Letters are a common form of written communication in both personal and professional contexts. Different types of letters are used for various purposes. Here are the main types of letters:

- **Formal Letters**: These are used in professional and official situations. They are typically addressed to individuals or organizations and follow a standard format. Examples include job applications, business inquiries, and complaints.
- **Informal Letters**: These are used for personal or casual situations. They are less formal and can be addressed to friends, family, or acquaintances. Examples include thank-you notes, personal messages, and casual inquiries.
- **Personal Letters**: These are written for personal use and can be informal in nature. Examples include letters to family members, friends, or pen pals.
- **Official Letters**: These are used for governmental or organizational purposes. They are formal and often require adherence to specific protocols. Examples include legal documents, notices, and public correspondence.

### Formats of Letters

The format of a letter is crucial for its clarity and professionalism. A well-structured letter should have the following components:

- **Header**: This includes the sender's address, date, and recipient's address.
- **Salutation**: The greeting, which is usually formal (e.g., "Dear Mr. Smith") or informal (e.g., "Hi John").
- **Body**: The main content of the letter, divided into paragraphs.
- **Closing**: A formal or informal sign-off, followed by the sender's name.
- **Enclosures**: Any additional documents or materials attached to the letter.

### Qualities of a Good Letter

A good letter should be clear, concise, and well-structured. Here are some qualities to consider:

- **Clarity**: The letter should convey its message clearly and concisely.
- **Professionalism**: Use appropriate language and tone, and ensure proper spelling and grammar.
- **Relevance**: The content should be relevant to the purpose of the letter.
- **Effectiveness**: The letter should achieve its intended purpose, whether it is to inform, persuade, or request something.
