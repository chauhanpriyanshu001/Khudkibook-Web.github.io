Analysis of Advanced Machining Processes Syllabus — Unit-wise Content

The official GTU curriculum for Analysis of Advanced Machining Processes (subject code ME02000511) as prescribed for M( · Semester 2 divides the course into 5 units, covering 50 listed topics. The unit-wise breakdown below is read from the GTU syllabus document for this subject code, so you can check what the course actually covers before deciding which papers to attempt.

Download the official syllabus PDF (ME02000511)

Unit 1 Introduction to Advanced Machining Processes (AMP)

8 lecture hours

  • Need for advanced machining processes
  • Classification of AMP: Mechanical
  • Chemical, and Electrochemical
  • Comparison of AMP with conventional machining
  • Applications in aerospace
  • automotive, and tool manufacturing

Unit 2 Mechanical Advanced Machining Processes

8 lecture hours

  • Abrasive Jet Machining (AJM): Working principle
  • abrasive selection
  • process parameters
  • material removal mechanism
  • Water Jet Machining (WJM) & Abrasive Water Jet Machining (AWJM): Principles
  • nozzle design
  • fluid dynamics
  • effects of process parameters
  • Ultrasonic Machining (USM): Mechanism of material removal
  • tool design
  • effect of amplitude
  • frequency, and slurry composition

Unit 3 Electrochemical and Chemical Machining Processes

10 lecture hours

  • Electrochemical Machining (ECM): Working principle
  • electrode design
  • electrolyte selection
  • process analysis
  • advantages, and applications
  • Electrochemical Grinding (ECG): Mechanism
  • applications in precision machining Chemical Machining (CHM): Etching techniques
  • chemical milling
  • photochemical machining
  • material compatibility

Unit 4 Thermal Energy-Based Machining Processes

12 lecture hours

  • Electric Discharge Machining (EDM): Working principle
  • spark erosion mechanism
  • dielectric fluid selection
  • tool design
  • process optimization
  • Wire EDM (WEDM): Process setup
  • wire selection
  • process control parameters
  • Laser Beam Machining (LBM): Laser types
  • process characteristics
  • heat- affected zone
  • industrial applications
  • Plasma Arc Machining (PAM): Plasma generation
  • cutting mechanism
  • Electron Beam Machining (EBM): Principle
  • vacuum requirements
  • energy conversion
  • applications in micro-machining

Unit 5 Modelling, Simulation, and Optimization of AMP

7 lecture hours

  • Analytical and empirical modelling of Advanced Machining Processes (AMP) Finite Element Method (FEM) in machining analysis
  • Multi-objective optimization techniques (Taguchi
  • Response Surface Methodology
  • Genetic Algorithm) Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) in jet-based machining Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning in machining process optimization
