### 2.1.1. Nouns

- **Proper Nouns:** Refer to specific people, places, or things, such as "London," "John," or "Sunday."

**Example:**

- **Personal Pronouns:** First person singular (I, me), first person plural (we, us), second person singular and plural (you), third person singular (he, she, it) and plural (they, them).

- **Example:** She is writing a letter now.
- **Example:** They are reading a book.

### 2.2.1.3. Present Perfect Tense
The **Present Perfect Tense** is used to describe actions that have happened at an indefinite time in the past but are relevant to the present. It is formed by using "have" or "has" followed by the past participle (verb + -ed).

- **Subject + have/has + verb (past participle)**
- **Example:** I have written a letter.
- **Example:** They have read the book.

The **Present Perfect Continuous Tense** is used to describe actions that started in the past and have continued up to the present. It is formed by using "have" or "has" followed by the present participle (verb + -ing).

## 2.2.3. Future Tense (Simple)

### Introduction
The **future tense** is used to describe actions or events that will happen in the future. It is essential for expressing intentions, predictions, and plans. The simple future tense is formed using the auxiliary verb **will** followed by the base form of the main verb.

### Formation of Future Tense
- **Positive Statements:** 
  - Subject + will + base form of the verb + object.
  - **Example:** She will go to the library tomorrow.

- **Negative Statements:**
  - Subject + will + not + base form of the verb + object.
  - **Example:** They will not go to the park today.

- **Questions:**
  - Will + subject + base form of the verb + object?
  - **Example:** Will you come to the meeting tomorrow?

### Usage in Different Contexts
- **Intention or Plan:** 
  - The future tense can be used to express intentions or plans.
  - **Example:** I will finish my project by next week.

- **Prediction:** 
  - It can also be used to make predictions.
  - **Example:** It will rain tomorrow.

- **Promise or Offer:** 
  - The future tense can be used to make promises or offers.
  - **Example:** I will help you with your homework.

### Example
> **Example:** 
> - **Positive Statement:** She will go to the library tomorrow.
> - **Negative Statement:** They will not go to the park today.

## 2.4. Subject- Verb Agreement

Subject-verb agreement is a fundamental aspect of English grammar. It involves ensuring that the subject and verb of a sentence agree in number (singular or plural). This agreement is crucial for clear and correct communication.

### 2.4.1. Defining Subject and Verb
- **Subject:** The subject is the person, place, thing, or idea that is doing or being something in the sentence. It is the part of the sentence that performs the action or is being described.
- **Verb:** The verb is the action word or state of being in the sentence. It indicates what the subject is doing or being.

### 2.4.2. Singular and Plural Subjects
- **Singular Subject:** A single person, place, thing, or idea. Examples: "The cat," "John," "The book."
- **Plural Subject:** More than one person, place, thing, or idea. Examples: "The cats," "John and Mary," "The books."

### 2.4.3. Regular and Irregular Verbs
- **Regular Verb:** Verbs that form their past tense and past participle by adding -ed to the base form. Examples: "walked," "talked."
- **Irregular Verb:** Verbs that do not follow the standard -ed rule. Examples: "walked," "talked."

### 2.4.4. Subject-Verb Agreement Rules
1. **Singular Subject with Singular Verb:**
   - **Example:** "The dog barks."
2. **Plural Subject with Plural Verb:**
   - **Example:** "The dogs bark."
3. **Singular Subject with Collective Noun and Plural Verb:**
   - **Example:** "The committee are discussing the issue."
4. **Singular Subject with Non-Singular Verb:**
   - **Example:** "The news is on the television."
5. **Compound Subject with Plural Verb:**
   - **Example:** "John and Mary are going to the park."

### 2.4.5. Subject-Verb Agreement with Prepositional Phrases
- A prepositional phrase can come between the subject and the verb, but the subject remains the same.
   - **Example:** "The book on the table is new."
   - **Example:** "The students in the class are from different countries."

### 2.4.6. Subject-Verb Agreement with Indefinite Pronouns
- **Indefinite Pronouns:** Words like "each," "everyone," "anyone," "someone," "everybody," "nobody," "somebody," "anybody."
   - **Example:** "Each student has completed the assignment."
   - **Example:** "Everyone in the room is happy."

### 2.4.7. Subject-Verb Agreement with Collective Nouns
- **Collective Noun:** A noun that refers to a group of people or things but can be treated as a singular or plural subject depending on the context.
   - **Example:** "The team is ready to play."
   - **Example:** "The team are divided on the issue."

### 2.4.8. Subject-Verb Agreement with Indefinite Quantifiers
- **Indefinite Quantifiers:** Words like "some," "many," "few," "much."
   - **Example:** "There is much to be done."
   - **Example:** "There are many books on the shelf."

### 2.4.9. Subject-Verb Agreement with Compound Subjects
- **Compound Subject:** A subject that consists of two or more subjects joined by "and," "or," "nor," "but," "for," "so," "yet."
   - **Example:** "John and Mary are going to the park."
   - **Example:** "Neither the students nor the teacher is late."

> **Example:**  
> "The students and teachers are going to the picnic.  
> The teachers and students are going to the picnic."

## 2.5. Basic Sentence Patterns of English (Explanation of S, V, O, A, C)

Basic sentence patterns in English are essential for forming clear and grammatically correct sentences. These patterns are categorized by the roles of the words in the sentence: subject (S), verb (V), object (O), adverbial (A), and complement (C).

### 2.5.2. Sentence Pattern S+V
- **Structure:** Subject + Verb
- **Explanation:** This pattern is the simplest form of a sentence where the subject is performing the action described by the verb.
