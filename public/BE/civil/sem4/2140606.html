Numerical And Statistical Methods for Civil Engineering Syllabus — Unit-wise Content

The official GTU curriculum for Numerical And Statistical Methods for Civil Engineering (subject code 2140606) as prescribed for Civil Engineering · Semester 4 divides the course into 10 units, covering 40 listed topics. The unit-wise breakdown below is read from the GTU syllabus document for this subject code, so you can check what the course actually covers before deciding which papers to attempt.

Download the official syllabus PDF (2140606)

Unit 1 Reorientation

3 lecture hours

  • Definition of probability
  • Exhaustive events
  • Pair wise independent events
  • Multiplicative law of probability
  • Conditional probability
  • Baye’s theorem

Unit 2 Probability Distributions

5 lecture hours

  • Random variable
  • Mathematical Expectation
  • Standard Deviation
  • Poisson and Normal distributions
  • Mode Statistics

Unit 3 Descriptive Statistics

3 lecture hours

  • Standard deviation

Unit 4 Correlation and Regression

4 lecture hours

  • Bivariate distribution
  • Correlation coefficients
  • Regression lines
  • Formulas for Regression coefficients
  • Rank correlation

Unit 5 Curve Fitting

3 lecture hours

  • Fitting of Linear
  • Exponential and Logarithmic curves
  • Least squares method Numerical Methods

Unit 6 Finite Differences and Interpolation

8 lecture hours

  • Finite Differences
  • Backward and Central operators
  • Interpolation by polynomials: Newton’s forward
  • Backward interpolation formulae
  • Gauss & Stirling’s central difference formulae
  • Newton’s divided and Lagrange’s formulae for unequal Intervals

Unit 7 Numerical Integration

3 lecture hours

  • Newton-Cotes formula
  • Trapezoidal and Simpson’s formulae
  • error formulae
  • Gaussian quadrature formulae

Unit 8 Solution of a System of Linear Equations

3 lecture hours

  • Gauss elimination
  • partial pivoting
  • Gauss-Jacobi and Gauss- Seidel methods

Unit 9 Roots of Algebraic and Transcendental Equations

10 lecture hours

  • false position
  • Secant and Newton-Raphson methods
  • Rate of convergence

Unit 10 Numerical solution of Ordinary Differential Equations

15 lecture hours

  • 06 Taylor series method
  • Euler method
  • Runge-Kutta method of order four
  • Milne’s Predictor-Corrector method
