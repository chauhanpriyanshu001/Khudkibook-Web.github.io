## 5.3. Properties of sound and light waves.

### 5.3.1 Properties of Sound Waves
**Sound Wave:** A sound wave is a mechanical wave that propagates through a medium by causing the medium particles to vibrate back and forth in the direction of wave propagation. Sound waves can travel through gases, liquids, and solids.

- **Wavelength (λ):** The distance between two consecutive compressions or two consecutive rarefactions.
- **Frequency (f):** The number of vibrations or cycles per second, measured in Hertz (Hz).
- **Amplitude (A):** The maximum displacement of a particle from its equilibrium position.
- **Speed (v):** The rate at which the wave travels through the medium, given by v = λ f.

**Example:**
> **Example:** A sound wave has a frequency of 1000 Hz and a wavelength of 0.343 m. Calculate the speed of the wave.
>
> Given:
> f = 1000 Hz
> λ = 0.343 m
>
> Speed v = λ f = 0.343 m × 1000 Hz = 343 m/s.

### 5.3.2 Properties of Light Waves
**Light Wave:** A light wave is an electromagnetic wave that travels through space at the speed of light, c. Unlike sound waves, light waves can travel through a vacuum.

- **Wavelength (λ):** The distance between two consecutive crests or troughs.
- **Frequency (f):** The number of vibrations or cycles per second, measured in Hertz (Hz).
- **Amplitude (A):** The maximum displacement of the electric and magnetic fields from their equilibrium positions.
- **Speed (v):** The speed of light in vacuum, approximately 3 × 10⁸ m/s.

**Example:**
> **Example:** A light wave has a wavelength of 500 nm. Calculate its frequency.
>
> Given:
> λ = 500 nm = 500 × 10⁻⁹ m
>
> Speed of light c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s
>
> Frequency f = c λ = 3 × 10⁸ m/s 500 × 10⁻⁹ m = 6 × 10¹⁴ Hz.

## 5.4. Phase, phase difference and various terms of wave equation (𝑦 = 𝐴𝑠𝑖𝑛(𝜔𝑡 + 𝜑)) [ derivation of equations of velocity and acceleration is not required]

### 5.4.1 Phase and Phase Difference
**Phase (𝜙):** The phase of a wave is the position of a wave relative to a reference point. It is expressed in radians or degrees.

- **Phase Difference (Δ𝜙):** The difference in phase between two waves, indicating their relative positions.
- **Phase Angle (𝜔𝑡 + 𝜙):** The argument of the sine function in the wave equation y = A (ω t + φ).

**Example:**
> **Example:** Two waves are represented by y₁ = A (ω t + φ₁) and y₂ = A (ω t + φ₂). Calculate the phase difference between the two waves.
>
> Given:
> φ₁ = 60^
> φ₂ = 120^
>
> Phase difference Δ φ = φ₂ - φ₁ = 120^ - 60^ = 60^.

### 5.4.2 Wave Equation and Its Terms
The general form of a wave equation is given by:
[ y = A \sin(\omega t + \phi) ]

- **Amplitude (A):** The maximum displacement of the wave.
- **Angular Frequency (𝜔):** The rate of change of the phase with time, given by ω = 2π f.
- **Phase Angle (𝜙):** The initial phase of the wave.

**Example:**
> **Example:** A wave is described by the equation y = 3 (4π t + π 4 ). Determine the amplitude, angular frequency, and phase angle of the wave.
>
> Given:
> y = 3 (4π t + π 4 )
>
> Amplitude A = 3
>
> Angular frequency ω = 4π rad/s
>
> Phase angle φ = π 4.

### 5.4.3 Velocity and Acceleration of a Wave
- **Velocity (v):** The rate of change of displacement with respect to time.
- **Acceleration (a):** The rate of change of velocity with respect to time.

The velocity of a wave is given by:
[ v = \omega ]

The acceleration of a wave is given by:
[ a = -\omega^2 y ]

**Example:**
> **Example:** A wave is described by the equation y = 2 (5t + π 6 ). Calculate the velocity and acceleration of the wave at time t = 0.
>
> Given:
> y = 2 (5t + π 6 )
>
> At t = 0:
> y = 2 ( π 6 ) = 2 × 0.5 = 1
>
> Angular frequency ω = 5 rad/s
>
> Velocity v = ω = 5 m/s
>
> Acceleration a = -ω² y = -(5²) × 1 = -25 m/s².
